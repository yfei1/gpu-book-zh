Details
  • 16 B microbenchmark and path boundaries: 16 B bandwidth-sweep microbenchmark (not gemv) streaming 4 GiB from shared HBM across all 132 SMs concurrently (data sits in shared HBM, and which stack holds which bytes is not specified; 4 GiB far exceeds the 60 MiB shared L2, so reads pass through the central L2 band with no reuse; K = 16 B loads in flight per thread, verified in machine code), reaching a measured plateau of about 4.50–4.55 TB/s (4.548 TB/s max median).
  • Little's-law mean round-trip upper bound: computed as peak bytes in flight ÷ measured bandwidth (since peak ≥ time-averaged bytes in flight, the mean round trip is at most this value), starting at about 0.33 µs unloaded; wait stage and service order along the path were not measured.
  • 16 KiB/SM point (K = 1): reaches 3.89 TB/s (85.5% of plateau), with mean round-trip upper bound ≤ 0.56 µs.
  • 24 KiB/SM knee and knee band: the K = 1 95%-of-plateau knee sits at 24 KiB/SM, reaching 4.34 TB/s (95.4% of plateau) with mean round-trip upper bound ≤ 0.75 µs; K = 1 caps at 32 KiB/SM, and the K = 1..16 95%-of-plateau knee band spans 24–40 KiB/SM.
  • 64 KiB/SM point: reaches ≥ 98.4% of plateau across all K ≥ 2 series (enough, not the minimum), with mean round-trip upper bound about 1.9 µs.
  • Step 3 comparison with gemv v1: although v1 fills all 2,048 resident threads/SM, each thread issues only 1 × 2 B W load per iteration to HBM (while x hits in L1/L2), keeping just 4.0 KiB/SM in flight—about 6× below the 24 KiB/SM knee (2 B curve and single-iteration round trip not measured).
