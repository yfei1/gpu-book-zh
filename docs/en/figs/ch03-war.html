Issuing a shared-memory load only submits a request, so the empty-slot barrier must wait until the load lands in registers, or a load delayed by contention lets the next round overwrite unread data and silently corrupt the sum.

Details
  • Single-slot two-barrier handoff: one 16,384 B (16 KiB) SMEM buffer slot sss is handed off across round rrr and round r+1r+1r+1 by two 8 B mbarrier objects, full[s] and empty[s], displaying arrivals left, bytes left, and phase, with arrivals left atomically resetting to its initial expected count at every phase flip.
  • Expected consumer arrivals: this schematic draws 1 consumer warp making 1 leader arrival so empty[s] expects 1 arrival, whereas a real kernel with KKK consumer warps sharing the slot initializes empty[s] to KKK—for example K=16K = 16K=16 in Chapter 8's expert-kernel weight ring, 1 lane 0 arrival per warp.
  • Wide shared-memory load latency: issuing a wide 16 B shared-memory load (512 B per warp, about 32 cycles unloaded conflict-free dependent latency, 32.06 cycles measured) only submits a request.
  • Mode 1 (Safe Order, safe Pattern 1): waits until pending shared-memory loads hit 0, finishes the multiply-add, and runs an intra-CTA fence (fence.acq_rel.cta) before arriving on empty[s]; safety comes from pending loads already sitting in registers (pending = 0) before arrival, whereas the intra-CTA fence is not a cross-proxy fence and only prevents compiler sinking and makes the arrival instruction wait for loads (standalone stress-test count and fence-only timing delta for drawn safe Pattern 1 were not measured standalone).
  • Safe Pattern 2 paired benchmark: Chapter 8's Stage 6 routed-expert kernel paired benchmark uses safe Pattern 2 (arriving after another activation-ring full wait with no fence), cutting overwrites from 551 in about 4.58 million calls to 0 in about 4.40 million calls and slowing Stage 6 by about 0.4–1.6 µs (% share not measured).
  • Mode 2 (Early Arrival, write-after-read / WAR hazard): flips empty[s] while 4 pending shared-memory loads remain in flight, so whenever a load returns late under contention, next-round async-proxy TMA overwrites first-written sample word w0 first (a write-after-read / WAR hazard), loading next-round data into r0 and silently corrupting acc.
  • Schematic notes and timeline scale: single-SM view (1 of 132), 1 consumer warp, 4 × 512 B sample spacing, address-order 512 B TMA batch countdown, issue-order load returns, and other-slot gaps are schematic; the bottom timeline shows step order only, not to cycle scale.
